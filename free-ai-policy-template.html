AI use policy

[Business name] · Last reviewed [date] · Owner: [name]

This policy explains how we use AI tools in [Business name]: what's fine, what isn't, and who to ask. It applies to everyone who works with us, including part-timers and freelancers. If you're not sure, ask before you use it, not after.

1. The tools we use

We've approved these AI tools for work: [e.g. ChatGPT Business, Microsoft Copilot, Claude Team]. Use your work account, not a personal or free one, for anything to do with customers. Anyone wanting to try a new AI tool for work checks with [name] first.

2. What AI can help with

  • Drafting emails, posts, quotes and documents for a person to check
  • Summarising long documents, emails and meeting notes
  • Research and ideas, which we check before relying on
  • [add jobs specific to your business]

3. What never goes into an AI tool

  • Customers' personal details unless the tool is on our approved list for that job
  • Bank details, card numbers, passwords or login codes
  • Health information or anything else sensitive about a person
  • [anything confidential to your business or your clients]

4. A person checks before it goes out

AI makes mistakes and says them confidently. Anything AI produces for a customer, whether an email, quote, post or reply to a review, is read by a person before it's sent. The person who sends it is responsible for it.

5. AI that does jobs on its own

Some jobs run automatically. For each one we write down what it does, what it's allowed to do without asking, and who checks it.

  • Jobs AI can do on its own: [e.g. sort enquiries, draft follow-ups for approval]
  • Jobs that need a person to sign off first: [e.g. sending invoice reminders, replying to reviews]
  • Never: agree refunds or discounts, promise dates or prices, or make a decision about someone's job, credit or account
  • [name] checks what the automated jobs have done every [week / month]

6. Being upfront with customers

If an AI answers our phone or web chat, it says so at the start and offers a way to reach a person. If we ever use AI to make a significant decision about someone, we tell them, let them have their say, offer a person to review it and let them challenge it. For emails and posts drafted with AI, our position is: [e.g. we don't label them, but a person always writes or checks them].

7. If something goes wrong

If you think customer information has gone somewhere it shouldn't, or AI has sent something it shouldn't have, tell [name] straight away. Nobody gets into trouble for owning up quickly.

8. Reviewing this policy

We look at this policy every six months, or sooner if we start using AI for something new or the rules change. Next review: [date].
