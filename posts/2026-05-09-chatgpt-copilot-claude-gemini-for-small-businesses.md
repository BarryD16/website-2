When we first wrote this guide in May, the question most people were asking was which AI tool writes the best email. Six months on, that's no longer the question worth asking.

All four of the big names are now built into software that small businesses already pay for, and all four have moved on from writing drafts to doing jobs, like chasing an invoice, pulling figures out of your accounts or tidying up a list of leads. So the more useful question is which one comes with what you already use, and what you're happy to let it do.

We've rewritten this guide with UK prices checked in November 2026. They change often, so treat them as a guide and check the provider's own page before you buy.

## The short answer

If your business runs on Microsoft 365 (Outlook, Word, Excel, Teams), start with **Copilot**, because Microsoft now sells it as part of its small business plans. If you're on Google Workspace, you already have **Gemini**, as it's included in the price. If you want an assistant that works across lots of different tools, such as your accounts package, your CRM and your inbox, **Claude** and **ChatGPT** are both strong, and Claude currently has the edge on ready-made connections to small business software.

Very few of us need to pick one forever. Start with whichever is already sitting inside software you pay for, and only add another if there's a job it can't do.

## ChatGPT

ChatGPT, made by OpenAI, is still the one most people have heard of, which helps when you're trying to get a team on board.

**What's changed:** there's now a cheaper paid plan, Go, at £7 a month, sitting between the free version and Plus at £20. The free version can search the web these days, so the old complaint about out-of-date answers mostly no longer applies. And since June, free and Go users in the UK see adverts at the bottom of some answers, chosen based on what you're chatting about. Plus, Business and Enterprise don't show adverts.

**What it's good at:** general writing, research, images, and increasingly jobs with several steps, using its agent features to work through a task rather than just answer a question.

**For a business:** once more than one person is using it for work, ChatGPT Business (from £15 per user per month on a yearly plan, or £18 monthly) gives you admin controls and keeps your business conversations out of the free, ad-supported version.

**(Caveat:)** the adverts are fine for personal use, but they're one more reason not to put customer details through a free account.

## Microsoft Copilot

Microsoft has made this much simpler for small businesses. Copilot Business is for firms with fewer than 300 people on a Microsoft 365 Business plan, and it has the same features as the version big companies pay more for.

**Cost:** £16.10 per user per month on top of your existing 365 licence, with an introductory price of £13.80 for the first year if you sign up before the end of December 2026. Or you can buy it bundled, with Business Standard plus Copilot at £18.10 per user per month.

**What it's good at:** summarising long email threads in Outlook, writing up Teams meetings, building Excel formulas and drafting Word documents from your own files. It works from the documents you already have access to, so its answers are grounded in your business rather than the internet.

**Who it suits:** anyone whose working day lives in Outlook and Excel.

**(Caveat:)** it's only as tidy as your files. If your shared drive is full of "Quote_final_v3_REAL.docx", Copilot will find all of them. It can also see anything a person has permission to see, so it's worth checking your sharing settings before you switch it on.

## Claude

Claude is made by Anthropic. In May we said it had fewer integrations than ChatGPT, and that has now flipped.

**What's changed:** Anthropic launched Claude for Small Business in May. It connects Claude to QuickBooks, PayPal, HubSpot, Canva, Docusign, Google Workspace and Microsoft 365, and a September update added more, including Xero, Shopify, Square, Stripe, Gusto and Zoom. Xero is the one we'd flag for UK businesses, given how many of us use it. There's no extra charge for any of this beyond a paid Claude plan.

**What it's good at:** long documents like policies and proposals, which has always been its strength, and now jobs like chasing overdue invoices, month-end prep, checking a contract or sorting new enquiries, with you approving before anything is sent, posted or paid.

**Cost:** Claude Pro is £18 a month (£15 a month if you pay yearly). The Team plan, which makes more sense once there's more than one of you, starts at £18 per user per month on a yearly plan.

**(Caveat:)** connecting it to your accounts means giving it real access. Start with jobs that only read, like a weekly summary of who owes you money, before letting it draft anything that goes to a customer.

## Gemini

Gemini is Google's AI, and this section was the most out of date in our original guide. Google stopped charging extra for it back in January 2025.

**What's changed:** Gemini now comes as part of Google Workspace business plans. Business Starter (£5.90 per user per month on a yearly plan) includes Gemini in Gmail and the Gemini chat app. Business Standard (£11.80) adds it across Docs, Sheets, Meet and the rest, plus a research tool that works from your own files.

**What it's good at:** drafting and sorting email in Gmail, summarising documents in Drive, and writing up Meet calls.

**Who it suits:** anyone already on Google Workspace. You're paying for it already, so it's worth ten minutes checking whether your team knows it's there before you buy anything else.

**(Caveat:)** Starter only gets the basics. If you want Gemini inside Docs and Meet, you'll need Standard.

## How to choose now

Rather than picking the "best" tool, three plain questions get you most of the way.

**What do you already pay for?** If it's Microsoft 365, look at Copilot first. If it's Google Workspace, use the Gemini you've already got. If your business mostly runs on separate tools (Xero for the books, a booking system, a CRM), Claude or ChatGPT will join them up better.

**What's the one job you want done?** Pick a real, recurring job rather than "use AI more". Chasing invoices, writing up quotes, answering the same five customer questions every week. Then look at which tool connects to the place that job lives. If your invoices are in Xero or QuickBooks, that points one way; if they're buried in Outlook, it points another.

**What should it be allowed to do?** This is the new question, and the important one. These tools can now send emails, post content and update records, not just suggest wording. A sensible starting rule is that AI drafts and a person approves, and customer information stays in a business plan rather than a free account. If you'd like help putting that in writing, our [AI governance and policy support](/ai-governance-policy-support.html) page explains how we do it.

## A note on using more than one

Plenty of businesses end up with two. You might use Copilot for day-to-day email and documents, and Claude to chase invoices out of Xero. That's completely reasonable, as long as everyone knows which tool is for what and what each one is allowed to touch.
