On a Monday night in November, I asked AI to check every price on my own blog. It went through the ChatGPT, Copilot, Claude and Gemini comparison I'd written in May, checked each figure against the providers' own pages, found that most of them had changed, rewrote the post in my voice and put it on a preview link. I read it there and published it. My part took about as long as reading it.

I'm not telling you that to show off. I'm telling you because it's a good example of the gap I see every week, between how most of us use AI and what it can actually be set up to do.

## How most of us use it

Most of us were introduced to AI as a clever chat box, so that's how we use it. Ask a question, copy the answer, paste it somewhere else. Write me an email. Summarise this. Give me ten ideas for a social post.

There's nothing wrong with that. It's where everyone starts, and it's genuinely useful. However, it's the smallest part of what these tools can do now, and it still leaves you doing the boring bit in the middle: the copying, the pasting, the remembering to do it at all.

## What my week actually looks like

My day job is designing fitted bedrooms and home offices, which means a lot of time in people's homes and a fair bit of admin around it. Alongside that I run Orchestrate Digital. I couldn't do both without AI taking care of the mundane. Here's what that looks like in practice.

**A briefing before I've started.** Every weekday morning a short brief lands in my inbox with the day ahead and the one customer worth calling today, picked from the last ten days. If there isn't a strong candidate, it says so rather than making one up, which I've come to appreciate more than I expected.

**Follow-ups I only have to read.** New enquiries go into a simple database. Each morning AI drafts a follow-up for every one and leaves it in the notes for me. I read each one, tweak it if needed, and send it myself. Nothing goes out without me.

**Writing that sounds like me.** I gave AI a guide to how I write: the phrases I use, the ones I never would, even how I punctuate. Emails and posts (this one included) start out sounding like me, so I spend my time on what to say rather than fixing how it's said.

**Notes that file themselves.** Ideas and reminders I record between appointments end up as tidy notes in my Google Drive, instead of lost somewhere on my phone.

**A sales coach in my pocket.** After a run of home visits, I talk through what worked and what didn't. It helps me sharpen my approach, spot what actually converts and get ready for the week ahead. It's no replacement for a good mentor, but it's there at ten o'clock at night when nobody else is.

**A website I run myself.** I can add posts from a simple editor, and AI does the fiddly checking, like those prices, and puts changes on a preview link. Nothing goes live until I've read it.

Add it up and I get back comfortably more than five hours a week. More importantly, it's the five hours I'd least like to spend, which leaves me free to focus on what matters: the customer in front of me, and the design that fits their home.

## What made the difference

It isn't a cleverer AI. I use the same tools you can buy for the price of a takeaway a month. The difference is in how they're set up, and it comes down to four things.

**Connected to where the work lives.** AI that can see my diary, my enquiries and my files doesn't need me to copy and paste everything into it.

**Given a job, not a question.** "Every morning, draft a follow-up for each new enquiry" is a job. It runs whether I remember or not.

**Told how I write.** Without a tone of voice guide, AI sounds like AI. With one, it sounds like me on a good day.

**A person signing off.** Everything that matters still comes past me before it goes out. That's the bit that makes it safe, and it's also the bit that means I trust it.

## Where it still falls short

It makes mistakes. It'll occasionally get a detail wrong or misread the tone of a situation, which is exactly why I read everything before it goes. Setting it up properly also takes time at the start, which is the bit most people never get round to.

And it isn't good at everything. When I wanted to add realistic finishing touches to a bedroom design image for a customer, the AI chat tools weren't up to it, so I use a proper image editing tool for that instead. Part of using AI well is knowing when not to.
